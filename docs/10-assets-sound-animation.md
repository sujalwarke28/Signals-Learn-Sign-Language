# 10 · Fonts, sound cues and the confetti animation

Every asset in the app is either an open-licence font or generated from a script
in [`tool/`](../tool/). Nothing is a silent stub and nothing needs a network fetch
at runtime.

---

## 10.1 Fonts

| File | Family | Used for | Licence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `assets/fonts/NunitoVariable.ttf` | Nunito | Body text, labels, buttons | SIL Open Font License 1.1 |
| `assets/fonts/Baloo2Variable.ttf` | Baloo2 | Headlines and titles | SIL Open Font License 1.1 |

Both are bundled rather than loaded through `google_fonts`, which means no
network request on first paint and the app renders identically offline. Nunito is
rounded and highly legible at small sizes; Baloo2 is chunky and playful, which is
what gives the headings their character without going childish.

They're declared in `pubspec.yaml` under `flutter: fonts:` and wired into the text
theme in [`lib/core/theme/app_theme.dart`](../lib/core/theme/app_theme.dart).

### Replacing them

Drop a TTF into `assets/fonts/`, update the `fonts:` block in `pubspec.yaml`, and
change the two constants `_display` and `_body` at the top of `AppTheme`.

## 10.2 Sound cues

Six short WAV files in `assets/sounds/`, all generated by
[`tool/generate_sounds.py`](../tool/generate_sounds.py):

| File | Length | When it plays | Character |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `tap.wav` | 0.09s | Buttons, cards, nav, chips | Barely-there wooden blip |
| `correct.wav` | 0.46s | Correct quiz answer | Rising major third + fifth |
| `incorrect.wav` | 0.42s | Wrong quiz answer | Soft falling two-tone — a nudge, not a buzzer |
| `complete.wav` | 0.75s | Video watched through; upload finished | Four-note major climb |
| `post.wav` | 0.29s | Forum post or reply sent | Quick two-note lift |
| `celebrate.wav` | 1.70s | Quiz passed; lesson published | Held chord with a sparkle run over it |

### They are synthesised, not sampled

The script builds each cue from scratch: additive sine partials with an
exponential decay envelope, which gives a soft struck-bell or marimba timbre.
That means they're original, royalty-free, and there's no attribution to track.
It also means they're **deliberately quiet and warm** — the brief was subtle, not
jarring, so nothing is percussive or bright.

Regenerate (e.g. after editing the pitches):

```bash
cd ~/Desktop/Signals
python3 tool/generate_sounds.py
```

Needs Python 3 only — `wave` and `struct` are standard library, and the tiny bit
of maths is `math`.

### Swapping in your own audio

Any short WAV or MP3 works. Keep the filenames, drop them in `assets/sounds/`,
and the `Sfx` enum picks them up unchanged. Keep them under a second or so —
these are interaction cues, not music.

### How playback avoids being annoying

[`lib/core/sound/sound_service.dart`](../lib/core/sound/sound_service.dart) does
three things that matter:

* **A pool of four players**, round-robin. A cue that's still ringing isn't cut off
  when the next one fires, so overlapping taps don't stutter.
* **A 70ms per-cue debounce.** Hammering a button doesn't stack the same sample on
  top of itself.
* **`mixWithOthers` audio focus.** The cues never duck or interrupt the lesson
  video's own audio — important, since a UI beep stealing focus mid-lesson would
  be worse than no sound at all.

There's a master switch in **Profile & settings → Interaction sounds**, persisted
with `shared_preferences`.

### Auditioning them

```bash
afplay assets/sounds/correct.wav        # macOS
```

Worth doing once — you're the judge of whether they're pleasant, and the pitches
are one line each to change in the script.

## 10.3 The confetti animation

`assets/lottie/confetti.json`, generated by
[`tool/generate_lottie.py`](../tool/generate_lottie.py).

46 particles, 110 frames at 60fps (about 1.8 seconds). Roughly half are small
rectangles ("ribbons") and half are round dots, in eight muted-but-cheerful
colours chosen to read well on both light and dark surfaces.

It's a real Lottie file, hand-generated rather than downloaded: each particle is a
shape layer whose position keyframes **sample a projectile trajectory** — outward
velocity, gravity, and a drag coefficient, integrated frame by frame in Python.
That's why the burst arcs upward and falls rather than fanning out in straight
lines, which is what cheap confetti animations look like. Each particle also has
its own spin, a scale-up on birth, and a fade near the end of its life.

Regenerate:

```bash
python3 tool/generate_lottie.py
```

Tweakable constants at the top of the script: `N` (particle count), `OP` (total
frames), `PALETTE`, and the `g` / `drag` values inside `trajectory()`.

### Where it fires

[`lib/widgets/celebration.dart`](../lib/widgets/celebration.dart) wraps a screen
in a `CelebrationScope`; any descendant can call `celebrate()`. Right now the
results screen fires it once, on a pass, alongside `celebrate.wav`.

Replacing it with a downloaded Lottie is a drop-in: overwrite the JSON file.

## 10.4 The rest of the motion

No asset needed for these — they're `flutter_animate`, implicit animations and
Hero transitions:

| Where | What |
| --- | --- |
| Lesson card → detail | `Hero` on the artwork, keyed `lesson-art-<id>` |
| Correct answer | Scale pop with an `elasticOut` settle |
| Wrong answer | Short horizontal shake |
| Every tap target | `Pressable` dips to 96.5% scale under the finger |
| Progress ring and bars | `TweenAnimationBuilder` sweeps from 0 on first build |
| Lists | Staggered fade + rise, capped at 8 items so long lists don't crawl |
| Streak flame, empty-state icons | Gentle infinite loops |
| Badges | One-off `elasticOut` pop when earned |
| Page transitions | Material 3 `FadeForwardsPageTransitionsBuilder` |

## 10.5 Colour

There are no hand-picked hex values for the main palette. One seed colour —
`#6C5CE7`, a friendly violet — goes through `ColorScheme.fromSeed()` and Material
3's tonal palette generator produces every surface, container and on-colour for
both light and dark. That's what keeps the contrast sane and stops any
eye-searing combinations.

On top of that there's a small set of semantic tokens (`AppColors`: streak amber,
success green, wrong red) with separate light and dark values, and one tint per
lesson category. Those are chosen rather than generated, because they carry
meaning that shouldn't shift with the seed.
